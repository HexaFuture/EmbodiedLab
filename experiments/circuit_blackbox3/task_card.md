# Identify a sealed three-terminal circuit

**Goal.** Identify which of these candidate networks is installed in the box and determine every component value in it (ohm, farad).

| Name | Between A and C | Between C and B | Required parameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | R1 | R2 | R1_ohm, R2_ohm |
| T2 | R1 | C1 | R1_ohm, C1_F |
| T4 | R1 and C1 in parallel | R2 | R1_ohm, R2_ohm, C1_F |

Resistors and capacitors are ideal linear elements (a capacitor does not leak). C is a terminal label; C1 is a capacitor. The box is opaque.

**Apparatus.** A circuit board, a component tray, a DC supply, a sealed box and a two-channel logger unit stand on the table; find each of them with the calibrated cameras. `get_task_spec().apparatus` lists every part and how to recognise it.

- *Board.* A dark green plate with brass node pads; column numbers and row numbers are printed on its edges. Each pad has one socket toward each neighbouring pad: a square conical recess with 45° walls that guides a contact pin in. Everything with a pin or a terminal on one pad is connected there; pads are otherwise insulated from each other.
- *Fixed terminals.* The supply's + terminal is strapped to one board pad by a red strap and its − terminal to another by a black strap. The box (near-black, printed BOX) has terminals A (red), B (black) and C (green), each strapped to a board pad by a strap of its colour. If the box contains a capacitor, it is discharged when the episode starts.
- *Blocks.* Every two-pin component is a rigid block that bridges two neighbouring pads: a base with a spherical contact pin under each end, and on top a handle or, on a meter, a display head that serves as its handle. Blocks: two voltmeters `V1`, `V2` (dark blue-grey display heads) and one ammeter `A` (dark red display head), each head printed with its name in white and with a red band at its + end, logger channels `CH1` (yellow handle) and `CH2` (cyan handle) with a red band at the + end of the base, resistors R_s printed `1k`, `10k`, `100k` ({rs_nominal_ohm_text} Ω nominal, each within ±{rs_tolerance} relative), a switch `SW` (blue, a rocker on top printed I and O: pressing the I end down closes it, the O end opens it; it starts open) and nine wires (grey, no resistance). The wires are identical and unnumbered; any wire may go in any well printed W. Up to four blocks can meet at one pad; two blocks cannot share the same pair of sockets. The + pin of a polarised block (meters, channels) is the end with the red band; printing on a block reads from its − end toward its + end.
- *Changeover* `CO`. A grey block that bridges three pads in a straight line, with pins marked `T1`, `C` and `T2` on its base: the T1 and C pins sit in the facing sockets of the first two pads, the T2 pin in the third pad's socket that faces the middle pad (the middle pad's other socket under the block stays free). A yellow lever on top selects the throw: pressing its raised end down tips it to that side and connects C to the throw on that side (T1 or T2); while it moves between them C is connected to neither (break before make). It starts on T1.
- *Tray.* A green-grey tray with one labelled well per block: `V1`, `V2`, `A`, `CH1`, `CH2`, `1k`, `10k`, `100k`, `SW`, `W` on each wire's well, and a long well printed `C/O` for the changeover. Every block starts standing in its own well.
- *Supply.* A grey unit printed DC SUPPLY with an ON/OFF push button (travel {supply_unit_button_travel_m} m; it acts once {supply_unit_acts_at_m} m down). Each press toggles the output ON or OFF; it starts OFF. While OFF its terminals are disconnected. The green ON lamp shows the output is on; the amber CC lamp lights while the output current is at its limit. Set point {supply_v_set} V (actual within ±{supply_v_tolerance} relative); current limit {supply_current_limit_a} A: above it the supply holds the current at the limit and its voltage drops (CC).
- *Logger unit.* A dark blue unit printed LOGGER with a keypad (keys `0` to `9`, `RATE`, `CLR`, `REC`; same travel and acting depth as the supply button) and a display: CH1 live (volts, refreshed every {logger_display_refresh_s} s), SET (the entered record length in s), RATE (Hz), NO (the record being made or the last one finished), LEFT (records left), a red REC lamp and an ERR lamp.

**Instruments.**
- *Voltmeters* V1, V2: 4½ digits (±{voltmeter_counts} counts), autoranging {voltmeter_ranges_text} (resolution {voltmeter_resolution_text}), input resistance {voltmeter_input_ohm} Ω between the pins; they show V(+) − V(−).
- *Ammeter* A: 4½ digits, autoranging {ammeter_ranges_text} (resolution {ammeter_resolution_text}); between its pins it is a resistance of {ammeter_shunt_ohm_text} Ω on these ranges respectively; it shows the current entering its + pin.
- Each meter converts once every {voltmeter_conversion_s} s and holds the last conversion on its display, with the range's unit lit on the screen beside the digits; noise is about half a count rms. A meter changes range within a conversion (up above {autorange_up_above_counts} counts, down below {autorange_down_below_counts} counts); above the top range it shows `OL`. A meter off the board shows zero.
- *Logger* channels CH1, CH2: V(+) − V(−) between the channel block's pins, input resistance {logger_input_ohm} Ω each, range {logger_range_v_text} V, resolution {logger_resolution_v} V, noise about {logger_noise_v} V rms; a channel off the board reads 0 V. To record: key the length in seconds ({logger_record_s_text}, two digits at most; CLR clears the entry), press RATE to step through {logger_rates_hz_text} Hz (120 at the start), then REC. The record starts at once and runs its full length; keys do nothing while it runs. At most {logger_max_records} records per episode. ERR lights for a length out of range, when no record is left, or when the record would not finish within the budget. Record n is evidence `<episode_id>:logger:<n>` (NO on the display); `export_logger(evidence_id)` returns a finished record: CSV `sample_id, t_s, status, ch1_V, ch2_V` (t_s is simulation time since the episode start) with its SHA-256.
- *Observations.* Every `get_observation` is evidence of the state it shows: it returns `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`). Readings are taken from the displays in its images.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- `measurements` lists the readings you used, each citing one piece of evidence: an observation with the readings its displays show (`V1`, `V2`, `A`, or `CH1` from the logger display), or a logger record with a window [t0_s, t1_s] and the mean of `CH1` and/or `CH2` over the samples in it. Values in volts and amperes. At least one entry has `used_in_fit: true`.
- Readings are genuine: every cited display reading is within {counts} counts of what that display showed in that observation (it must show a number); every logger window lies inside the record, holds at least 2 samples, all `ok`, and each cited mean is within {logger_mV} mV of the mean of those samples.
- Measurements are valid: for every entry used in the fit, the box is in a closed circuit (an external path joins two of its terminals that does not run through a voltmeter or logger channel input) or a capacitor in it holds at least 0.5 V at the observation (for a window: throughout it, or from the lever throw, switch toggle or supply press on when it holds one), every block on the board is seated (a block counts as on the board from when it is seated there until it stands in a tray seat again; blocks in the tray are judged only by leaving the bench in order, and moving them is not a change), nothing changed on the bench during the window except at most one lever throw, switch toggle or supply ON/OFF press (no block placed on or removed from the board, no meter range change) and every cited display has converted since the last such change, every cited instrument is seated on the board, and an instrument (cited or not) seated on the board measures the box: across two box terminals, in series with the box, across a resistor R_s in series with the box, across two box terminals and the ammeter in series with the box, or across the supply while a resistor R_s is in series with the box.
- Leave the bench in order before you submit: supply OFF, every block back in a tray seat labelled for its kind (V1 or V2 for a voltmeter, CH1 or CH2 for a channel, any W for a wire, the changeover in `C/O`, otherwise its own label), a capacitor in the box at no more than {max_capacitor_v} V, the logger not recording, the robot holding nothing; and no loose item tipped over (the direction that pointed up at the start now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top).
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A block is seated when every pin is within {seating_pin_to_socket_m} m of its socket (two facing sockets of two neighbouring pads; for the changeover as described above), the bottom of its base is within {seating_base_gap_m} m of the board surface, it is tilted at most {seating_tilt_deg}° and the robot has released it. Only seated blocks are part of the circuit; a block that lies anywhere else is open.
- A block stands in a tray seat when it is within {seating_tray_xy_m} m of the seat centre on the seat floor, tilted at most {seating_tray_tilt_deg}°, and released.
- The circuit is solved again whenever a block is seated or leaves its seat, the lever changes throw, the switch or the supply changes, or the ammeter changes range. Between such changes a capacitor's voltage follows the circuit continuously; everything else responds at once. A capacitor in the box does not leak: it discharges only through what it is connected to, inside or outside the box.
- Grasping follows device_calibration.gripper_mechanism: a block is held once both fingers close on its handle (or display head) and stop.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.
