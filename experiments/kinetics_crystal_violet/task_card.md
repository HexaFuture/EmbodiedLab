# Kinetics: reaction orders and rate constant of crystal violet fading

**Goal.** Determine the reaction order m in crystal violet, the reaction order n in hydroxide and the rate constant k of the reaction of crystal violet with hydroxide at {temperature_C} °C:
CV⁺ (violet) + OH⁻ → CVOH (colourless).
k is defined by rate = k [CV⁺]^m [OH⁻]^n with m and n the integer orders you report (concentrations in mol/L, time in s); its unit follows from m and n.

**Reagents.** Three bottles feed the dispensing station: `CV STOCK`, crystal violet, c = {c_stock_uM} μM (30 mL; the label value is exact); `WATER`, deionised water (60 mL); `NaOH`, sodium hydroxide {naoh_label_M} mol/L (certificate ±{naoh_certificate_pct}%, 30 mL). Crystal violet absorbs most at {lambda_max_nm} nm; its molar absorptivity in this batch is not published. Crystal violet and NaOH react as soon as they touch, wherever both are present. Without stirring a cuvette holds layers in order of addition: each addition's jet mixes completely with an equal volume of the liquid at the top (all of it if there is less), and adjacent layers then exchange liquid only slowly, 0.005 mL/s across each interface. MIX mixes the cuvette fully. Liquid can only enter a cuvette from the dispensing station; it cannot be poured from one container into another.

**Apparatus.** A rack of {cuvette_count} empty cuvettes (teal collars; the cuvettes are numbered 1–9 on their rims, the rack wells 1–9 on their collars), the dispensing station (dark base) and the spectrophotometer (blue housing). Every place a cuvette stands (rack wells, the orange station seat, the spectrophotometer's cell well) is a raised square collar with a chamfered well: a cuvette lowered onto the collar centre stands in the well.
- Cuvettes: light path {cuvette_path_cm} cm, capacity {cuvette_capacity_mL} mL. A reading needs at least {cuvette_min_read_mL} mL in the cuvette. Liquid added beyond the capacity overflows and is lost. The minimum for a reading and the capacity apply to the liquid actually delivered, which differs from the nominal volumes by the dispensing scatter: a fill of exactly {cuvette_min_read_mL} mL can end up just short of the minimum, and one of exactly {cuvette_capacity_mL} mL just over the capacity. Each cuvette carries a filling funnel whose white rim is the gripper handle (the short neck on the rim is the filling port), and a stir bar.
- Dispensing station: each bottle line has a coarse button ({dispenser_coarse_mL} mL, dark blue) and a fine button ({dispenser_fine_mL} mL, light blue), the volume printed on each cap. Each bottle carries a paper label with its name (`CV STOCK`, `WATER`, `NaOH`), and each line's name is also printed on the station with its pair of buttons. Dispensed liquid falls through the spouts into the cuvette on the orange seat. One press dispenses its nominal volume × (1 + {dispenser_repeatability} N(0, 1)) within {dispenser_dispense_s} s; unless the same cuvette stands on the seat when the button registers and when the flow ends, the liquid goes to the drip tray. The green `MIX` button stirs the seated cuvette for {mixer_mix_s} s (removing it earlier voids the mixing).
- Spectrophotometer: single beam, {spectrophotometer_wavelength_nm_text} nm, bandwidth {spectrophotometer_bandwidth_nm} nm. It starts at an arbitrary wavelength between 400 and 700 nm. Type the wavelength on the keypad and press `ENT` (`CLR` clears the entry; an amber `SET` mark shows while digits are typed); each key's label is printed on its cap. `BLANK` (violet) stores the light through the cuvette in the well as the reference at the current wavelength; `READ` (red) shows A = log10(I_ref / I_sample) ({spectrophotometer_display_decimals} decimals), each in {spectrophotometer_measure_s} s. Without a BLANK at the current wavelength readings use the factory reference and carry an instrument baseline; after changing the wavelength the screen shows `BLANK` until you blank again. The screen shows the wavelength, the last absorbance, that prompt, the digits being typed, `Err` after an invalid entry and `----` while measuring, nothing else. Stray light ≤ 0.1 % of the incident light; against a BLANK, A_measured = −log10((10^(−A) + s)/(1 + s)) with s the stray light as a fraction of the light passing the blanked cuvette, s ≤ 0.0012; noise σ_A = {spectrophotometer_noise_sigma_A}.
- Screen: a dark glass on the spectrophotometer housing with green seven-segment digits. One row shows the wavelength (three digits and `nm`; while you type, the digits being typed; `Err` after an invalid entry), the other the absorbance (a sign place and four digits, the decimal point after the first digit; `----` while measuring). Two amber marks: `BLANK` (the prompt) and `SET` (digits being typed). After a BLANK the absorbance row shows 0.000. No tool reports what the screen shows; the screen is seen in the camera images.
- `KIN` (lime key, its name printed on its cap): timed readings of the cuvette in the well at the current wavelength, one every {spectrophotometer_kin_interval_s} s for {spectrophotometer_kin_duration_s} s; pressing `KIN` again ends the run early. While a run is going the screen shows `----`, `BLANK` and `READ` are ignored, digits and `CLR` still edit the entry, and `ENT` shows `Err` (the wavelength stays).
- Buttons and keys are pressed with the arm: they travel {buttons_travel_mm} mm and switch at {buttons_switch_mm} mm down; every cap has a raised pad.

**Tools.** `export_spectro_kinetics()` returns every KIN run so far (`run, wavelength_nm, blank_wavelength_nm`, readings `point, t_s, A_display`, `t_s` in simulated seconds since the episode started; CSV with SHA-256), the points of a run still going included; cite a run as `<episode_id>:kinetics:<run>`. `<episode_id>` is `episode_id` in task_spec.json. A run's `blank_wavelength_nm` names the wavelength of the last BLANK before it started (empty before the first); the run was taken against that BLANK only if it equals the run's `wavelength_nm`.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- Readings genuine: every run you cite is a KIN run of this episode, every point you list with it (`point`, `A`) is a point of that run and its `A` equals the logged `A_display`. Each run's `c_OH_M` and `c_CV_uM` are the concentrations as dispensed, before any reaction (from the nominal volumes and the labels), and agree within {composition_tol_pct}% with those of the cuvette each listed point measured. A point of a cuvette that had spilled or overflowed cannot be cited.
- Measurement valid: the cited points include points of two cuvettes holding both crystal violet and NaOH at different [OH⁻] (in the same run or in different runs), the higher [OH⁻] at least {oh_margin_pct}% above the lower. Every run a reported quantity names in `derived_from` is one of the cited runs.
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) is tipped over (the direction that pointed up when the episode started now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Restore the bench: when you submit, every cuvette stands in a rack well (any well), the cell well and the station seat are empty, no KIN run is going, and the robot holds nothing.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A cuvette counts as standing in a well (rack, station seat or cell well) only when it is not held, its release complete, tilted no more than 10°, and within 4 mm horizontally and 3 mm vertically of the well floor.
- A dispense lands in a cuvette only if that cuvette stood on the station seat when the button registered and still stands there when the flow ends; otherwise it goes to the drip tray. A press for more than the bottle still holds dispenses nothing.
- BLANK and READ measure whatever cuvette stands in the cell well when they finish; presses while one runs are ignored. Every BLANK discards the references stored at other wavelengths.
- The beam passes through the liquid between 1.4 and 1.6 mL above the cuvette floor (by volume); an unmixed cuvette is read through the layer(s) in that band: the reading is their mean transmittance, each layer weighted by the share of the band it fills.
- A cuvette tilted more than 45°, held or knocked over, spills all its contents at once. Once a cuvette has spilled or overflowed, its dispensing history no longer describes what it holds, for the rest of the episode, even after refilling.
- A key or button registers once when its cap reaches the switch depth, and again only after the cap has risen to within 2 mm of rest. The keypad keeps at most 3 digits; an invalid `ENT`, or `ENT` while a measurement or a KIN run is going, shows `Err` and keeps the wavelength; `Err` clears at the next key press.
- The dosing lines, the spouts and the neck on each funnel rim have no collision; each bottle, with its pump and the riser of its dosing line, stands inside one invisible collision column.
- Each KIN point reads whichever cuvette stands in the cell well at that moment.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** e_rel = |k − k_true| / k_true, where k_true is the rate constant defined with the true orders. Success when all conditions hold and e_rel ≤ {threshold_pct}%. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e_rel / {threshold}). G is 1 when both reported orders equal the true orders and every acceptance condition holds (including restoring the bench), and 0 otherwise.
