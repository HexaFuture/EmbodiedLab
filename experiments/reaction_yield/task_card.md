# Reaction yield: the best conditions for an intermediate product

**Goal.** Make one batch of the product P from 1.00 mL of `S STOCK` with the highest yield you can. Choose the block temperature, the amounts of R STOCK and BUFFER and how you run the batch, make one product cuvette from exactly 1.00 mL of S STOCK, leave it in the rack and report its yield. Yield = moles of P in the product cuvette ÷ moles of S dispensed into it.

**Chemistry.** In solution S reacts with the reagent R:
S + R → P (the product you want),
P + R → Q (P reacts further with R),
S + R → D (a side path),
2 P → X (P pairs up; R catalyses this and is not used up).
The quench K (the `QUENCH` line) destroys R at once, one K for one R; once a cuvette's R is gone nothing in it reacts any more.
The rates and activation energies of the four reactions are not published; the first three are first order in each reactant, the pairing is second order in P and first order in R, and every rate constant rises with temperature. S (yellow), P (red) and Q (violet) absorb in the visible; R, D, X and K are colourless. S, P and R are each stable on their own: nothing reacts without R. The reactions run in any cuvette that holds R together with S or P, wherever it stands, at the temperature of its liquid, until you submit.

**Reagents.** Four bottles feed the dispensing station: `S STOCK`, c = {c_S_uM} μM (30 mL); `BUFFER` (60 mL); `R STOCK`, c = {c_R_uM} μM (30 mL); `QUENCH`, K at c = {c_K_mM} mM (30 mL). The label values are exact. Cuvette {p_standard_cuvette} stands in the rack filled with {p_standard_volume_mL} mL of `P STD`, the product P at {c_P_uM} μM (exact); P's molar absorptivity in this batch is not published. All liquids in the bottles are at room temperature, {room_C} °C. Without stirring a cuvette holds layers in order of addition: each addition's jet mixes completely with an equal volume of the liquid at the top (all of it if there is less), and adjacent layers then exchange liquid only slowly, 0.005 mL/s across each interface. MIX mixes the cuvette fully. Liquid can only enter a cuvette from the dispensing station; it cannot be poured from one container into another.

**Apparatus.** A rack of {cuvette_count} cuvettes (teal collars; the cuvettes are numbered 1–9 on their rims, the rack wells 1–9 on their collars), the dispensing station, the spectrophotometer and a dry block heater. Every place a cuvette stands (rack wells, the station seat, the spectrophotometer's cell well, the block's wells) is a well with a chamfered mouth: a cuvette lowered onto the centre of the mouth stands in the well.
- Cuvettes: light path {cuvette_path_cm} cm, capacity {cuvette_capacity_mL} mL. A reading needs at least {cuvette_min_read_mL} mL in the cuvette. Liquid added beyond the capacity overflows and is lost. The minimum for a reading and the capacity apply to the liquid actually delivered, which differs from the nominal volumes by the dispensing scatter. Each cuvette carries a filling funnel whose white rim is the gripper handle, and a stir bar.
- Dispensing station: each bottle line has a coarse button ({dispenser_coarse_mL} mL, dark blue) and a fine button ({dispenser_fine_mL} mL, light blue), the volume printed on each cap; each line's name is printed on the station with its pair of buttons. Dispensed liquid falls through the spouts into the cuvette on the station seat. One press dispenses its nominal volume × (1 + {dispenser_repeatability} N(0, 1)) within {dispenser_dispense_s} s; unless the same cuvette stands on the seat when the button registers and when the flow ends, the liquid goes to the drip tray. The green `MIX` button stirs the seated cuvette for {mixer_mix_s} s (removing it earlier voids the mixing).
- Dry block: an aluminium block with four cuvette wells numbered 1–4 and five keys. Four change the set point by −10, −1, +1 and +10 °C (each key's step printed on its cap), within 20–80 °C; it starts set to {room_C} °C. `LOG` (dark grey) switches the block's data logger on and off; it starts off. Its screen shows SET (the set point), BLOCK (the block's temperature, 0.1 °C; accuracy ±0.5 °C) and a `LOG` mark while the logger records. The block heats at up to {block_heat_C_per_s} °C/s and has no cooling: above the set point it cools down by itself. A cuvette in a well takes on the block's temperature; anywhere else it takes on the room's. Liquids poured together take their volume-weighted mean temperature.
- Spectrophotometer: single beam, {spectrophotometer_wavelength_nm_text} nm, bandwidth {spectrophotometer_bandwidth_nm} nm; its cell well is at room temperature. It starts at an arbitrary wavelength between 400 and 700 nm. Type the wavelength on the keypad and press `ENT` (`CLR` clears the entry; an amber `SET` mark shows while digits are typed). `BLANK` (violet) stores the light through the cuvette in the well as the reference at the current wavelength; `READ` (red) shows A = log10(I_ref / I_sample) ({spectrophotometer_display_decimals} decimals), each in {spectrophotometer_measure_s} s. Without a BLANK at the current wavelength readings use the factory reference and carry an instrument baseline; after changing the wavelength the screen shows `BLANK` until you blank again. Stray light ≤ 0.1 % of the incident light; noise σ_A = {spectrophotometer_noise_sigma_A}.
- `KIN` (lime key): timed readings of the cuvette in the well at the current wavelength, one every {spectrophotometer_kin_interval_s} s for {spectrophotometer_kin_duration_s} s; pressing `KIN` again ends the run early. While a run is going `BLANK` and `READ` are ignored.
- Screens: dark glass with green seven-segment digits. No tool reports what a screen shows; screens are seen in the camera images.
- Buttons and keys are pressed with the arm: they travel {buttons_travel_mm} mm and switch at {buttons_switch_mm} mm down; every cap has a raised pad.

**Tools.** `export_spectro_kinetics()` returns every KIN run so far (`run, wavelength_nm, blank_wavelength_nm`, readings `point, t_s, A_display`); `export_block_log()` returns the block logger's record: one row a second while `LOG` is on (`t_s`, `set_C`, `block_C`), nothing from while it is off. Times are simulated seconds since the episode started. `get_observation` returns an `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`) for what the screens show in its images; `<episode_id>` is `episode_id` in task_spec.json.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- Product as stated: `product_cuvette` is a cuvette you filled (not the P standard) that did not spill or overflow; its nominal dispensing history is `conditions` (S STOCK `S_mL`, R STOCK `R_mL`, BUFFER `buffer_mL`, each within 0.05 mL; QUENCH in any amount; nothing else) and `S_mL` is 1.00; it stood in a block well, and whenever it stood there the block was set to `block_set_C`.
- Readings genuine: every cited reading is an absorbance the spectrophotometer screen showed from a READ at the cited observation, of the cuvette the row names (`A` as shown). The readings include at least one of the product cuvette and at least one of the P standard while it still held only the standard.
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item is tipped over (more than 45° from upright) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Restore the bench: when you submit, every cuvette stands in a rack well (any well), the cell well, the station seat and the block wells are empty, no KIN run is going, the block's logger is off, its set point is at {room_C} °C or below (the block need not have cooled), and the robot holds nothing.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides):
- A cuvette counts as standing in a well only when it is not held, its release complete, tilted no more than 10°, and within 4 mm horizontally and 3 mm vertically of the well floor.
- A dispense lands in a cuvette only if that cuvette stood on the station seat when the button registered and still stands there when the flow ends; otherwise it goes to the drip tray. A press for more than the bottle still holds dispenses nothing.
- BLANK and READ measure whatever cuvette stands in the cell well when they finish. Every BLANK discards the references stored at other wavelengths.
- The beam passes through the liquid between 1.4 and 1.6 mL above the cuvette floor (by volume); an unmixed cuvette is read through the layer(s) in that band.
- A cuvette tilted more than 45°, held or knocked over, spills all its contents at once. Once a cuvette has spilled or overflowed, its dispensing history no longer describes what it holds.
- A key or button registers once when its cap reaches the switch depth, and again only after the cap has risen to within 2 mm of rest.
- The yield counted is the product cuvette's at the moment you submit.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** y is the product cuvette's yield when you submit, y* is a reference value: the highest yield that an offline search over set points, volumes and timings at the handling speed of this bench's robot found for a batch from 1.00 mL of S STOCK, taken within 600 s of S and R meeting; a yield above y* counts as y*. e = max((1 − y / y*) / {threshold}, |predicted_yield − y| / ({predicted_threshold} y)). Success when all conditions hold and e ≤ 1. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e), G is 1 when every acceptance condition holds (including restoring the bench) and 0 otherwise.
