# Electronics: the steepest active low-pass filter the kit allows

**Goal.** Build an active low-pass filter on the circuit board from the resistor and capacitor pieces, the wires and the two op-amp modules, driven by the frequency-response analyzer's output and measured with its probe at the filter's output. Its passband ripple over {objective_passband_low_hz} Hz to {objective_passband_high_hz} Hz (the largest deviation of the gain from the gain at {objective_passband_low_hz} Hz) must be at most {objective_ripple_max_db} dB; within that, make the stopband attenuation (the gain at {objective_passband_low_hz} Hz minus the highest gain from {objective_stopband_hz} Hz to {objective_stopband_high_hz} Hz, i.e. the smallest attenuation over that band) as large as you can. Leave your filter on the board, take a final sweep of it and report its attenuation and ripple.

**Apparatus.** A circuit board, two component trays and a frequency-response analyzer stand on the table; find each of them with the calibrated cameras. `get_task_spec().apparatus` lists every part and how to recognise it.

- *Board.* A dark green plate with brass node pads; column numbers and row numbers are printed on its edges. Each pad has one socket toward each neighbouring pad: a square conical recess with 45° walls that guides a contact pin in. Everything with a pin or a terminal on one pad is connected there; pads are otherwise insulated from each other, except the pads of one edge row, which a printed copper strip joins into one node, the ground rail (printed GND). One pad is printed IN.
- *Analyzer.* A grey unit printed FREQUENCY RESPONSE ANALYZER with a keypad and a screen. Its signal output is wired to the IN pad by a red lead, its ground to the ground rail by a black lead. The output is a sine of {analyzer_source_emf_v} V amplitude (EMF) through a source resistance of {analyzer_source_ohm} Ω, driving the IN pad against ground.
- *Pieces.* Every two-pin component is a rigid block that bridges two neighbouring pads: a base with a spherical contact pin under each end and a handle on top. Resistors: tan handles with colour bands, printed with their value, {resistors_nominal_text} Ω ({resistors_copies} of each, k = kΩ, 4k7 = 4.7 kΩ), each within ±{resistors_tolerance_pct} % of its value. Capacitors: blue film capacitors printed with their value, {capacitors_nominal_text} ({capacitors_copies} of each; n = nF, 2n2 = 2.2 nF), each within ±{capacitors_tolerance_pct} %. The two pieces of one value look alike but are two different parts. {wires} wires (grey, no resistance; identical and unnumbered). Pieces are not polarised. Up to four blocks can meet at one pad; two blocks cannot share the same pair of sockets.
- *Op-amp modules.* Two dark grey modules printed OPAMP 1 and OPAMP 2, each a straight three-pin block that sits on three neighbouring pads in a line, its pins IN+, IN−, OUT in that order along it (each pin's name is printed on the module). The board edge joining its IN− and OUT pads lies under the module. Each is a micropower op-amp with internal supplies: a large DC gain, a gain-bandwidth product of about {opamp_gbw_approx_hz} Hz (each module its own) and an output resistance that is not zero; it does not clip at the analyzer's level.
- *Probe.* The analyzer's input, a two-pin block with a yellow handle printed PROBE and a red band at the + end of its base. It reads V(+) − V(−) across its pins; its input is {analyzer_probe_input_ohm} Ω in parallel with {analyzer_probe_input_pf} pF.
- *Trays.* Two green-grey trays with one labelled well per block: the pieces' values, `W` on each wire's well, `PROBE`, and a long well for each op-amp module. Every block starts standing in its own well.

**Instrument.**
- *Keypad.* Keys `0` to `9`, `START`, `STOP`, `PTS`, `CLR`, `SWEEP` (travel {keys_travel_m} m; a key acts once {keys_acts_at_m} m down). A digit is appended to the entry (at most {analyzer_entry_digits} digits; a digit also clears `ERR`). `START`, `STOP` or `PTS` sets that field to the entry (Hz for START and STOP, an integer number of points for PTS) and clears the entry; with no entry it does nothing. A value is accepted when START and STOP are within {analyzer_freq_range_hz_text} Hz, START < STOP and PTS is within {analyzer_pts_range_text}; otherwise `ERR` lights and the field keeps its value. `CLR` clears the entry and `ERR`. `SWEEP` starts a sweep with the fields shown; if it could not end within the remaining budget, `ERR` lights and nothing starts. At the start START = {analyzer_default_start_hz}, STOP = {analyzer_default_stop_hz}, PTS = {analyzer_default_pts}. Keys do nothing while a sweep runs.
- *Sweep.* PTS frequencies spaced evenly in log f from START to STOP, both ends included, lowest first, one point every {analyzer_point_s} s. Each point is the probe's reading over the source EMF at that frequency, in its steady state: gain 20 log10|V/EMF| in dB (rounded to {analyzer_gain_decimals} decimals; below {analyzer_floor_db} dB it reads {analyzer_floor_db}) and phase in degrees ({analyzer_phase_decimals} decimals); the frequencies are rounded to {analyzer_freq_decimals} decimals. The readings carry no noise. Seating a block on the board or taking one off while a sweep runs does not stop the sweep: each point reads the circuit as the board is when that point is taken.
- *Screen.* START (Hz), STOP (Hz), PTS, NO (the sweep shown), ENTRY, a green RUN lamp while a sweep runs, an orange ERR lamp (lit only by a rejected keypad entry or a refused sweep), and the gain trace (dB, {analyzer_freq_range_hz_text} Hz on a log axis) of the running sweep or the last one.
- *Records.* Sweep n is evidence `<episode_id>:sweep:<n>`. `export_sweeps()` returns every sweep of this episode that has ended: its `evidence_id`, status (`complete`), when it started and ended (simulated s), START, STOP, PTS, the points measured, the rows (frequency Hz, gain dB, phase deg), a CSV of them with its SHA-256; and whether a sweep is running now.
- *Observations.* Every `get_observation` is evidence of the state it shows: it returns `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`).

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- `sweep_evidence` cites a complete sweep of this episode with START ≤ {objective_passband_low_hz} Hz and STOP ≥ {objective_stopband_high_hz} Hz; when it started, the probe's − pin was on ground and its + pin on neither ground nor the IN pad's node; nothing was seated on the board or taken off it after it started.
- `circuit` lists every block seated on the board when you submit, and nothing else: each block with its pads (`"column,row"`): the two pads of a piece or wire, the probe's + pad then its − pad, an op-amp module's IN+, IN−, OUT pads. Either id of two pieces of one value, and any wire id, names that part.
- The circuit on the board when you submit (with the probe where it is) has a passband ripple of at most {objective_ripple_max_db} dB, computed with the true values of its parts.
- Leave the bench in order before you submit: no sweep running, the robot holding nothing, every block that is not on the board back in a tray well labelled for its kind (a piece's value, any `W` for a wire, `PROBE`, either long well for a module); and no loose item tipped over (the direction that pointed up at the start now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top). Your filter stays on the board.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A two-pin block is seated when every pin is within {seating_pin_to_socket_m} m of its socket (two facing sockets of two neighbouring pads), the bottom of its base is within {seating_base_gap_m} m of the board surface, it is tilted at most {seating_tilt_deg}° and the robot has released it. An op-amp module is seated the same way with its IN+ and IN− pins in the two facing sockets of one edge and its OUT pin in the socket of the next pad along the same line facing IN−. Only seated blocks are part of the circuit; a block that lies anywhere else is open.
- A block stands in a tray well when it is within {seating_tray_xy_m} m of the well centre on its floor, tilted at most {seating_tray_tilt_deg}°, and released.
- The circuit is solved exactly at each frequency (linear, steady state): every seated part, the source resistance and the probe's input included.
- Grasping follows device_calibration.gripper_mechanism: a block is held once both fingers close on its handle and stop.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.**
- a is the stopband attenuation of the circuit on the board when you submit (the gain at {objective_passband_low_hz} Hz minus the highest gain from {objective_stopband_hz} Hz to {objective_stopband_high_hz} Hz; true part values, the probe where it is).
- Two reference values, both found offline for this instance with a passband ripple of at most {objective_ripple_max_db} dB. A_ref, the success line: the largest stopband attenuation of a filter of a standard design built from this kit's pieces and modules; a larger a counts as A_ref. A*, the ranking: the largest stopband attenuation that an offline search over any circuit this kit's pieces, wires and modules can build on this board found.
- e = max(max(0, A_ref − a) / {threshold} dB, |stopband_attenuation_db − a| / {reported_threshold} dB).
- Success when all conditions hold and e ≤ 1: a is at most {threshold} dB below A_ref, and the reported attenuation is within {reported_threshold} dB of a.
- Attainment (reported beside the score, it does not decide success): a − A* in dB; above 0 when your filter beats the search.
- Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e).
