# Mechanics: design, build and load the strongest truss bridge on a pegboard

**Goal.** Design and build a bridge on the pegboard from the bars of the kit, of at most {mass_limit_g} g, that spans the supports A and B and includes the load pin C. Then load it yourself on the loading frame, weight by weight, until it breaks. Your result is the largest load your bridge held before it broke.

**Apparatus.**
- **Pegboard.** A flat black board drilled with a grid of holes; the mouth of each hole is chamfered. Three steel pins stand fixed in holes of its middle row: the supports, marked A and B with yellow support symbols (A holds its pin in every direction, B, on rollers, only across the span), and the red load pin C between them; a red arrow at C shows the direction in which C will be pulled. The other holes are open.
- **Loose pins.** {loose_pins} steel pins stand in the holes of a pin stand, a small black plate drilled like the board. Each has an upright post with a pointed tip, a collar, and a shank below the collar. A pin goes into any open hole of the board shank first, until its collar rests on the board, and can be pulled out and moved again. A pin standing in a hole of the board is a joint of the bridge.
- **Bars.** 3D-printed flat bars with an eye at each end, lightening holes along the web and a raised grip knob in the middle. They come in four colours, blue, orange, green and white (printed from four different filaments, same outline), and four lengths; the length printed on a bar is the distance between its eye centres in mm (50, 71, 100 or 112). Bars of the same colour and length are identical. The kit: {kit_per_colour} in each colour. Masses (50 / 71 / 100 / 112): blue {mass_g_blue_50} / {mass_g_blue_71} / {mass_g_blue_100} / {mass_g_blue_112} g, orange {mass_g_orange_50} / {mass_g_orange_71} / {mass_g_orange_100} / {mass_g_orange_112} g, green {mass_g_green_50} / {mass_g_green_71} / {mass_g_green_100} / {mass_g_green_112} g and white {mass_g_white_50} / {mass_g_white_71} / {mass_g_white_100} / {mass_g_white_112} g. They start in the slots of wooden racks with wooden dividers, each slot's length printed at its end; a bar may go back into any slot. A bar is built into the bridge by slipping both of its eyes over pins standing in the board; several bars may share a pin, their eyes stacked on it. Each pair of pins takes at most one bar.
- **Scrap tray.** A galvanized steel tray marked SCRAP, for bars that failed and their pieces.
- **Loading frame.** A steel wire rope is fixed to the lower end of the load pin C (in a groove under the board) and leaves the board in the direction of the red arrow. It runs to a sheave at the foot of a guide frame of two aluminium columns, rises on one side of the columns, passes over a sheave at their top and comes down onto a carriage that slides on the columns. The carriage carries the load platform: an aluminium plate with a low rim, open above, on the other side of the columns from the rising rope. The rope's pull on C is the weight on the platform. The carriage and the empty platform weigh 1.0 kg. Below the platform two catch blocks stop it if the rope goes slack.
- **Weights.** Stackable knob weights in a wooden case lined with black rubber: 20 × 2 kg and 6 × 0.5 kg (polished steel) and 10 × 0.1 kg (brass), each marked with its mass, which is exact. Each weight is a cylinder with a knob on top and a round recess underneath that fits over the knob of another weight, so weights stand on the platform or stacked on each other. They start stacked by size in the case. The gripper takes a weight by its knob.
- **Load display.** A display in a grey housing, labelled LOAD: the rope's pull on C in N (one decimal), refreshed 10 times per simulated second.

**Instrument.**
- **Bar tester.** A fixed pin on a pedestal and a pin on a crosshead carriage that runs on a rail. Push the carriage's orange handle along the rail: a ball detent clicks it into the hole marked 50, 71, 100 or 112, where the two pins stand that many mm apart. Mount a bar by slipping its eyes over the two pins. On the console: the two-position MODE slider (TENSION / COMPRESSION; push its yellow post) and the green `START` key (travel {buttons_travel_mm} mm, switch at {buttons_switch_mm} mm).
- **Running a test.** `START` begins a run in the slider's mode with the bar mounted then. The tester's drive holds the carriage and moves it a few mm along the rail, away from the fixed pin (TENSION) or toward it (COMPRESSION): it takes up the play of the eyes on the pins and then pulls or pushes the bar along its length with a force rising at {tester_rate_n_s} N/s until the bar fails, and stops there. A bar fails in tension by breaking in two beside its grip knob: the carriage springs on a few mm and the two pieces stay on the pins, the knob on one of them. It fails in compression by buckling: it stays one piece, kinked upward beside its knob. A failed bar, or any piece of it, is useless from then on; the two pieces of a broken bar are not a mounted bar. A run with no bar mounted reads 0.0 for {tester_empty_stroke_s} s and ends. A bar taken off during a run ends the run (the bar stays intact). After a run the carriage stays where the run left it until nothing lies on the tester's pins; then the drive returns it to its hole and lets it go.
- **Display.** Line 1: the state (READY, RUN, DONE) and the mode. Line 2: FORCE, the present force (N, one decimal; 0.0 when not running). Line 3: PEAK, the force at which the last run ended, kept until the next `START`.
- **Observations.** Every `get_observation` is evidence of the state it shows: it returns `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`). Readings are taken from the display in its images. The tester has no logger and no export.

**Loading** (one loading per episode):
- The loading starts when the first weight touches the platform. From then on nothing may touch a bar or a pin of the bridge: not the robot, not anything it holds, not a weight.
- You may put weights on the platform and take them off again, one at a time, in any order and any amount.
- The bridge breaks when the rope's pull reaches the load at which it fails (below). The pull is the physical pull of the rope at every instant. A weight let go above the platform, or set down hard, pulls more than its weight for a moment, and so does pressing on the platform or on a weight. Such a moment can break the bridge, as it would on a real frame.
- When the bridge breaks, the episode ends at that instant: the running command ends with an error, the episode is closed (submitted with no arguments) and nothing is accepted after that. Then the bridge collapses, the rope goes slack and the platform drops onto its catch blocks.
- The held load is the largest LOAD reading while the bridge was intact and the platform was settled: the platform still for 0.5 s, and nothing of the robot (nor anything it holds) touching the platform or a weight on it. The empty platform counts if the bridge can carry it.
- You may end the episode without breaking the bridge by `submit_result`; the held load is then the largest settled reading so far. If no weight was ever put on the platform, the held load is 0.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- The bridge (the bars with both eyes on pins standing in the board when the loading starts) weighs at most {mass_limit_g} g.
- Leave the bench in order: when the episode closes, no loose item is tipped over or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top) or held by the robot; the weights are exempt (the robot may be holding one when the bridge breaks).
- Tidy up: when the loading starts, no bar or piece of a bar is on the tester's pins, every intact bar that is not in the bridge lies in a rack slot (any slot), every failed bar lies in the scrap tray (both pieces of a broken one), every loose pin stands in a hole of the board or of the pin stand, and the robot holds nothing but a weight. The bridge stays on the board.
- Hands off: from the start of the loading, nothing touches the bridge (see Loading).
- At most one `submit_result`; it closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A loose pin stands in a hole when it is not held, upright and down in the hole, its collar resting on the board. A bar is in the bridge when it is not held and both of its eyes are over two pins standing in different holes of the board (each eye's centre within 2 mm of a pin's axis, the eye down on the pin). Only the bars in the bridge count for its mass. Two bars that cross without sharing a pin are not joined where they cross; a bar cannot lie on its two pins if a third pin stands in its way.
- When the loading starts, the bridge as it stands then is the bridge that is loaded. It is analysed as a pin-jointed plane truss (each bar carries a force along its length only; the pins are frictionless joints). It fails at the load on C at which its first bar fails: a bar in tension breaks at its tensile strength, a bar in compression fails by crushing or buckling; these are properties of the bar's material (the compression a bar carries also depends on its length) and are printed nowhere. A bridge that is not rigid, or that leaves out A, B or C, carries nothing. A failed bar carries nothing.
- Each bar differs from its nominal properties by up to {bar_scatter_pct}% (manufacturing scatter).
- The rope and the bridge are an elastic line about 130 N/mm stiff (the platform sinks about 0.08 mm per kg); the rope's damping is an assumption of this simulation (about 3 % of critical with 20 kg on the platform).
- The force line refreshes {tester_refresh_hz} times per simulated second while a run is on; each value it shows carries its own Gaussian noise of {tester_noise_n} N (1σ), and so does the PEAK. An observation's images show the display as it is at that moment.
- The carriage takes about {carriage_detent_n} N along the rail to push it out of a hole and about {carriage_friction_n} N to slide it between holes; during a run the tester's drive holds it with about {carriage_drive_n} N more. `START` registers once when its cap reaches the switch depth, and again only after the cap has risen to within 2 mm of rest; with the slider between its positions it does nothing.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing is free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. At most one submission.

**Scoring.**
- H is the held load (see Loading); P* is a reference value: the largest failure load that an offline search found over the bridges this kit and the loose pins can build on this board within {mass_limit_g} g (at the bars' properties); an H larger than P* counts as P*.
- e = max(0, 1 − H / P*) / {threshold}.
- Success when all conditions hold and e ≤ 1: the bridge held at least (1 − {threshold}) × P*.
- Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e).
