# Normal modes of two coupled sliders

**Goal.** Determine both undamped normal-mode angular frequencies ω1 < ω2 (rad/s) of the coupled two-slider apparatus.

**Apparatus.** Two equal sliders ({slider_mass_kg} kg each) run on a common rail and are coupled to each other; they are the measurement channels CH1 and CH2, and each is labelled with its channel name. Each slider has a yellow grip post. On this bench the fingers have no contact hold: a post is held by finger friction only (the fingers close with at most 3 N each). On this bench the two fingers are not coupled: each has its own drive (at most 3 N) toward half the commanded width from the hand centre, so a finger that meets something stops there or pushes it along, while the other keeps closing toward its own target; the hand itself does not move. A slider that nothing holds is pulled back towards its encoder zero by its springs.

**Catches.** Each slider has a catch: a dark carriage on the rail, labelled with its slider's channel name, that moves only along the rail. A spring hook joins a slider to its catch: they hook by themselves when the slider meets its catch slowly, and a hooked slider and its catch move together. Each catch carries a blue push button labelled CLAMP; every press toggles the clamp. An amber lamp on the catch is lit while the catch is clamped and dark while it is free. A clamped catch cannot move. A free catch slides when pushed or carried and stays where it is left, also when its hooked slider's springs pull on it. A red pointer on each catch reads its position on a millimetre scale from −60 to +60 mm about the catch's zero mark (short marks every 1 mm, longer ones every 5 and 10 mm, numbers every 20 mm); the catch's travel covers its scale, and its zero mark is where its slider, hooked, stands at its encoder zero.

**Release post.** A yellow release post slides in one direction only and is returned to its rest position by a spring. Pulled at least 35 mm out of its rest position in a two-finger grip, while the recorder is READY, it opens both hooks at once and starts the keyed record; otherwise pulling it does nothing. The hooks stay open until that record has ended. A flat steel-grey link that moves with the post is not a handle.

**Damper lever.** An eddy-current damper: on a dark pedestal, a carriage with a yellow post slides between two spring detents printed OFF and ON. A dark-red magnet on the carriage stands over a copper vane at ON. At ON the damper brakes both sliders strongly (an oscillation settles within a few seconds); at OFF it has no effect; in between it brakes partly. It can be moved at any time.

**Recorder.** A keypad on a console labelled RECORDER with 12 push keys: the digits 0 to 9, CLR and a red REC key. Digits enter the record length in whole seconds (at most three digits), CLR clears the entry, and REC takes it: {recorder_record_s_text} s sets the recorder READY for a record of that length; any other entry shows ERR and does nothing. READY cannot be undone and every key is ignored until its record has ended; the release post starts the record, which then runs its full length. A record still running when the budget ends is lost. The recorder display, labelled RECORDER, shows a state word (IDLE, READY, REC, DONE or ERR) and three numbers: SET S (the entry being typed, or the keyed length), T S (seconds recorded so far during REC, the record's length at DONE) and NO (the number of the record being made or last made). The CH1 and CH2 POSITION MM displays show each slider's live encoder position.

At the start both catches are clamped at their zero marks, both sliders are hooked at their encoder zero, the damper lever is at OFF and the recorder is idle.

**Physics (public).** Each slider is held by its own restoring spring, and the two sliders are coupled to each other by a spring; the two local frequencies may differ. A small, equal viscous damping acts on both sliders. The quantities asked for are the undamped normal-mode frequencies of this coupled system.

**Instrument.** A dual-channel linear encoder: {recorder_sample_hz} Hz, {recorder_resolution_mm} mm resolution, range {recorder_range_mm_text} mm. Each channel reads its slider's displacement along the rail from its encoder zero, in the same sense for both channels (positive in the same direction as the catch scales). Records are numbered 1, 2, … in the order they are made. `export_recording(evidence_id)` returns a finished record: its `evidence_id`, the samples as rows and a CSV with the columns `t_s, x1_mm, x2_mm, status`, and the CSV's SHA-256. `t_s` is in simulated seconds from the physics tick at which the release opened the hooks, so the first sample (`t_s` = 0) holds the initial displacements. A sample's `status` is `OK`, or `OVER_RANGE` with `x1_mm` and `x2_mm` empty when a channel was outside the encoder range. Like a real encoder, the record does not say whether anything touched a slider or whether the damper acted during it; the evaluator checks the acceptance conditions from the bench's own log. `evidence_id` may also be the record's number (NO) or `"latest"`; the submission needs the full evidence id string that `export_recording` returns (a number or `"latest"` is not accepted there). No tool returns what a display shows: read the RECORDER display and the CH1 and CH2 POSITION MM displays in the camera images. On this bench the fingers have no contact hold: a post is held by finger friction only, and `device_calibration` has no `gripper_mechanism`. Every part of this bench (sliders, posts, catches, keys and lever) is jointed to the apparatus, so the bench has no loose items. `get_robot_state` reports `gripper_holding`: true while both fingers have pressed on the same post or slider with more than 0.02 N each for at least 0.1 s; it does not say which.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- One or more recordings, each cited once in `measurement_evidence_ids` by its full evidence id string.
- Each cited recording ran its full length and contains a clean stretch: at least 40 s of consecutive samples during which nothing gripped or touched a slider, the damper did not act and both channels were in range, with at least 1 mm peak-to-peak motion on both channels within the stretch. Gripping, contact or braking outside the stretch does not void the recording.
- The clean stretches show both modes: one stretch moves at least {min_minor_pp_mm} mm peak-to-peak along its minor principal axis, or stretches from two different recordings move along linearly independent principal directions (normalized |det| ≥ {min_abs_det}).
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) is tipped over (the direction that pointed up when the episode started now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Leave the bench as you found it: when you submit, both catches are clamped at their zero marks, both sliders are hooked and at rest at their encoder zero, the damper lever is at OFF, and the recorder is neither READY nor recording.
- `angular_frequencies_rad_per_s` gives ω1 and ω2 in rad/s, in strictly ascending order.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A push button or key acts once per press, when its cap is pressed down at least 4 mm (of its 9 mm travel); it acts again only after rising back above 2 mm.
- A slider hooks to its catch when their positions (each from its own zero) are within 1 mm of each other and their relative speed is at most 20 mm/s, except during a record. The hook holds the pair together with a stiff spring (up to 200 N). A free catch slides with a friction force of 0.6 N.
- The damper lever's detent spring pulls it to the nearer detent; it counts as at a detent within 4 mm of it. The damper adds a viscous deceleration of 8 s⁻¹ times the slider's speed in proportion to the magnet's overlap with the vane (full at ON, none at OFF).
- The release post counts as gripped only when both fingers press it with more than 0.02 N each, for at least 0.1 s. After a release it fires again only once it has been back within 5 mm of rest.
- Leaving the bench as you found it: a catch counts as at its zero mark, and a slider at its encoder zero, within {position_tol_mm} mm; a slider is at rest when it moves at most {max_speed_mm_s} mm/s.
- The principal axes of a clean stretch are those of its (x1, x2) samples about their mean: the major axis is the direction of largest spread, the minor axis is perpendicular to it. For the major-axis directions a and b of two stretches, normalized |det| = |a1·b2 − a2·b1| / (|a|·|b|), the sine of the angle between them.
- The drawn coupling and restoring springs, the scales, the displays and the printed labels are visual only; they have no collision. The catches, buttons, keys, console, lever and pedestal collide.
- A slider moved more than 0.2 mm off its rail ends the episode.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** e_rel = max over the two modes of |ω_submitted − ω| / ω. Success when all conditions hold and e_rel ≤ {threshold_pct}%. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e_rel / {threshold}).
