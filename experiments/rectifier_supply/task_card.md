# Rectified supply: load capability and filter choice

**Goal.** A bridge rectifier with a filter capacitor feeds a load. For each of the three capacitor blocks find the smallest load resistance R_min (Ω) at which the output, in its periodic steady state, still has a mean of at least {objective_mean_voltage_min_V} V and a peak-to-peak ripple of at most {objective_ripple_pp_max_V} V. Then build the final supply, a load of {objective_final_load_ohm} Ω with a capacitor block that meets both requirements, record its output with the logger and state the mean voltage and the ripple it delivers. The order is: record the final supply first, then put the bench in order, then submit.

**Circuit** (published; the component values are not). AC source {circuit_source_peak_V} V peak, {circuit_frequency_Hz} Hz, source resistance {circuit_source_resistance_ohm} Ω → bridge of four equal diodes (two conduct in series; Shockley law with I_s = {circuit_diode_is_A} A at {circuit_diode_T_K} K and an ideality factor between {circuit_diode_n_low} and {circuit_diode_n_high}) → the DC output, where the capacitor and the load are connected in parallel. R_min of a capacitor block is defined for this circuit with that block alone across the output and a load resistance R alone across it (any value of R from 150 Ω to 3300 Ω, not only the resistances of the blocks provided).

**Apparatus.** A circuit board, a component tray, the AC SOURCE + BRIDGE module and a two-channel logger unit stand on the table; find each of them with the calibrated cameras. `get_task_spec().apparatus` lists every part and how to recognise it.

- *Board.* A dark green plate with brass node pads; column numbers and row numbers are printed on its edges. Each pad has one socket toward each neighbouring pad: a square conical recess with 45° walls that guides a contact pin in. Everything with a pin or a terminal on one pad is connected there; pads are otherwise insulated from each other.
- *Source module.* A grey unit printed AC SOURCE + BRIDGE, holding the AC source and the bridge. Its DC output + is strapped to one board pad by a red strap and its − to another by a black strap. An ON/OFF push button (travel {supply_unit_button_travel_m} m; it acts once {supply_unit_acts_at_m} m down) toggles the source ON or OFF at each press; it starts OFF. While OFF the source is disconnected and the bridge conducts nothing; the green ON lamp shows it is on.
- *Blocks.* Every two-pin component is a rigid block that bridges two neighbouring pads: a base with a spherical contact pin under each end, and on top a handle or, on a meter, a display head that serves as its handle. Blocks: two voltmeters `V1`, `V2` (dark blue-grey display heads) and one ammeter `A` (dark red display head), each head printed with its name in white and with a red band at its + end, logger channels `CH1` (yellow handle) and `CH2` (cyan handle) with a red band at the + end of the base; capacitors printed `100uF`, `220uF`, `470uF` (blue handles, not polarised; nominal {capacitors_nominal_uF_text} µF, each within ±{capacitors_tolerance_pct} %); load resistors printed `150`, `330`, `470`, `680`, `820`, `1k`, `3k3` (tan handles; {load_nominal_ohm_text} Ω nominal, each within ±{load_tolerance_pct} %) and five wires (grey, no resistance). The wires are identical and unnumbered; any wire may go in any well printed W. Up to four blocks can meet at one pad; two blocks cannot share the same pair of sockets. The + pin of a polarised block (meters, channels) is the end with the red band; printing on a block reads from its − end toward its + end. Every capacitor is discharged at the start; one that is off the board keeps its charge.
- *Tray.* A green-grey tray with one labelled well per block: `V1`, `V2`, `A`, `CH1`, `CH2`, the capacitors' and resistors' printed values, and `W` on each wire's well. Every block starts standing in its own well.
- *Logger unit.* A dark blue unit printed LOGGER with a keypad (keys `0` to `9`, `RATE`, `CLR`, `REC`; same travel and acting depth as the module's button) and a display: CH1 live (volts, two decimals, refreshed every {logger_display_refresh_s} s), SET (the entered record length in s), RATE (kHz), NO (the record being made or the last one finished), LEFT (records left), a red REC lamp and an ERR lamp.

**Instruments.**
- *Voltmeters* V1, V2: 4½ digits (±{voltmeter_counts} counts), autoranging {voltmeter_ranges_text} (resolution {voltmeter_resolution_text}), input resistance {voltmeter_input_ohm} Ω between the pins; they show V(+) − V(−).
- *Ammeter* A: 4½ digits, autoranging {ammeter_ranges_text} (resolution {ammeter_resolution_text}); between its pins it is a resistance of {ammeter_shunt_ohm_text} Ω on these ranges respectively; it shows the current entering its + pin.
- Each meter converts once every {voltmeter_conversion_s} s and shows the mean of its quantity over that conversion (over a varying voltage or current it shows the average), holding it on its display with the unit printed on the head; noise is about half a count rms. A meter changes range within a conversion (up above {autorange_up_above_counts} counts, down below {autorange_down_below_counts} counts); above the top range it shows `OL`. A meter off the board shows zero.
- *Logger* channels CH1, CH2: the instantaneous V(+) − V(−) between the channel block's pins, input resistance {logger_input_ohm} Ω each, range {logger_range_v_text} V, resolution {logger_resolution_v} V, noise about {logger_noise_v} V rms; a channel off the board reads 0 V. To record: key the length in seconds ({logger_record_s_text}; CLR clears the entry), press RATE to step through {logger_rates_khz_text} kHz (1 kHz at the start), then REC. The record starts at once and runs its full length; keys do nothing while it runs. At most {logger_max_records} records per episode. ERR lights for a length out of range, when no record is left, or when the record would not finish within the budget. Record n is evidence `<episode_id>:logger:<n>` (NO on the display); `export_logger(evidence_id)` returns a finished record: CSV `sample_id, t_s, status, ch1_V, ch2_V` (t_s is simulation time since the episode start) with its SHA-256.
- *Observations.* Every `get_observation` is evidence of the state it shows: it returns `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`). Readings are taken from the displays in its images.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- `load_limits` gives one R_min per capacitor block; `evidence_ids` cites logger records or observations of this episode.
- Final supply: before you put the bench in order, take a logger record of the final supply's output with a channel across the output (its + pin on the output +) and give that record's evidence_id (`<episode_id>:logger:<n>`) as `final_configuration.evidence_id`. During that record: the source is ON; exactly one capacitor block is on the board, it is seated across the output and it is the one named in `final_configuration.module_id`; the output terminals are not joined; the resistance across the output (with the capacitor taken out, meters included) is within {objective_final_load_tolerance_pct} % of {objective_final_load_ohm} Ω, and so is `final_configuration.load_ohm`. The cited final record must use the logger's {min_rate_khz} kHz rate. Nothing on the board may change while it runs: no block seated on the board or taken off it, the source not switched, no meter changing range (taking a block from the tray, putting one into it or knocking one over there is not a change). The part of the record that begins at least {settle_s} s after the last change on the board must last at least {min_steady_s} s, and over that part the output must have a mean of at least {objective_mean_voltage_min_V} V and a peak-to-peak ripple of at most {objective_ripple_pp_max_V} V.
- Leave the bench in order before you submit: source OFF, every block back in a tray seat labelled for its kind (V1 or V2 for a voltmeter, CH1 or CH2 for a channel, any W for a wire, otherwise its own label), the logger not recording, the robot holding nothing; and no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) tipped over (the direction that pointed up at the start now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top).
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A block is seated when every pin is within {seating_pin_to_socket_m} m of its socket (two facing sockets of two neighbouring pads), the bottom of its base is within {seating_base_gap_m} m of the board surface, it is tilted at most {seating_tilt_deg}° and the robot has released it. Only seated blocks are part of the circuit; a block that lies anywhere else is open.
- A block stands in a tray seat when it is within {seating_tray_xy_m} m of the seat centre on the seat floor, tilted at most {seating_tray_tilt_deg}°, and released.
- The circuit is integrated continuously at {integration_hz} Hz; its connections are rebuilt whenever a block is seated or leaves its seat, the source is switched, or the ammeter changes range. Capacitors connected in parallel share their charge at once; a capacitor whose two pins are joined by a wire is discharged at once. The diodes have no reverse leakage or recovery; the capacitors do not leak.
- Grasping follows device_calibration.gripper_mechanism: a block is held once both fingers close on its handle (or display head) and stop.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** Each R_min against the true value for this instance's components (tolerance 1 %); `final_measurement.mean_voltage_V` against the mean of the final supply in its periodic steady state, for the configuration in the cited final record and this instance's components (0.5 %); `final_measurement.ripple_pp_V` against its peak-to-peak ripple (the larger of 0.01 V and 1 %). e is the largest error divided by its tolerance. Success when all conditions hold and e ≤ 1. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e).
