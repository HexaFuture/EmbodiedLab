# The wavelength of an unlabelled LED

**Goal.** Determine the peak emission wavelength λ_X (nm) of the LED marked `X`.

**Apparatus.** A circuit board, a component tray, a DC supply and a two-channel logger unit stand on the table; find each of them with the calibrated cameras. `get_task_spec().apparatus` lists every part and how to recognise it.

- *Board.* A dark green plate with brass node pads; column numbers and row numbers are printed on its edges. Each pad has one socket toward each neighbouring pad: a square conical recess with 45° walls that guides a contact pin in. Everything with a pin or a terminal on one pad is connected there; pads are otherwise insulated from each other.
- *Fixed terminals.* The supply's + terminal is strapped to one board pad by a red strap and its − terminal to another by a black strap.
- *LEDs.* Five LED blocks of one manufacturing process, at {temperature_C} °C. Four have tinted lenses printed with their peak emission wavelength in nm: `{leds_nm_L1}`, `{leds_nm_L2}`, `{leds_nm_L3}`, `{leds_nm_L4}` (L1 to L4); the fifth has a water-clear lens printed `X`. The lens tints can look alike in the cameras: the printed number, and the clear lens on X, are what tell the LEDs apart. The anode (+) of an LED is the end of its base with the red band; an LED conducts from anode to cathode and blocks the other way. An LED glows in its own colour while more than {glow_ma} mA flows through it.
- *Other blocks.* Every two-pin component is a rigid block that bridges two neighbouring pads: a base with a spherical contact pin under each end, and on top a handle or, on a meter, a display head that serves as its handle. Blocks: two voltmeters `V1`, `V2` (dark blue-grey display heads) and one ammeter `A` (dark red display head), each head printed with its name in white and with a red band at its + end, logger channels `CH1` (yellow handle) and `CH2` (cyan handle) with a red band at the + end of the base, resistors printed `100`, `330`, `1k` (tan handles; {rs_nominal_ohm_text} Ω nominal, each within ±{rs_tolerance} relative) and five wires (grey, no resistance). The wires are identical and unnumbered; any wire may go in any well printed W. Up to four blocks can meet at one pad; two blocks cannot share the same pair of sockets. The + pin of a polarised block (meters, channels, LEDs) is the end with the red band; printing on a block reads from its − end toward its + end.
- *Tray.* A green-grey tray with one labelled well per block: `V1`, `V2`, `A`, `CH1`, `CH2`, the LEDs' printed names, the resistors' printed values, and `W` on each wire's well. Every block starts standing in its own well.
- *Supply.* A grey unit printed DC SUPPLY with an ON/OFF push button and a SWEEP push button (each: travel {supply_unit_button_travel_m} m; it acts once {supply_unit_acts_at_m} m down). Each ON/OFF press toggles the output ON or OFF; it starts OFF. While OFF its terminals are disconnected. With the output ON, a SWEEP press sets the output to 0 V and raises it at {supply_sweep_v_per_s} V/s to the set point, where it stays; a SWEEP press during a sweep starts it again from 0 V; switching the output OFF ends a sweep (switched ON again it is at the set point); while the output is OFF, SWEEP does nothing. The green ON lamp shows the output is on; the amber CC lamp lights while the output current is at its limit. Set point {supply_v_set} V; current limit {supply_current_limit_a} A: above it the supply holds the current at the limit and its voltage drops (CC).
- *Logger unit.* A dark blue unit printed LOGGER with a keypad (keys `0` to `9`, `RATE`, `CLR`, `REC`; same travel and acting depth as the supply button) and a display: CH1 live (volts, refreshed every {logger_display_refresh_s} s), SET (the entered record length in s), RATE (Hz), NO (the record being made or the last one finished), LEFT (records left), a red REC lamp and an ERR lamp.

**Instruments.**
- *Voltmeters* V1, V2: 4½ digits (±{voltmeter_counts} counts), autoranging {voltmeter_ranges_text} (resolution {voltmeter_resolution_text}), input resistance {voltmeter_input_ohm} Ω between the pins; they show V(+) − V(−).
- *Ammeter* A: 4½ digits, autoranging {ammeter_ranges_text} (resolution {ammeter_resolution_text}); between its pins it is a resistance of {ammeter_shunt_ohm_text} Ω on these ranges respectively; it shows the current entering its + pin.
- Each meter converts once every {voltmeter_conversion_s} s and holds the last conversion on its display, with the unit printed on the head; noise is about half a count rms. A meter changes range within a conversion (up above {autorange_up_above_counts} counts, down below {autorange_down_below_counts} counts); above the top range it shows `OL`. A meter off the board shows zero.
- *Logger* channels CH1, CH2: V(+) − V(−) between the channel block's pins, input resistance {logger_input_ohm} Ω each, range {logger_range_v_text} V, resolution {logger_resolution_v} V, noise about {logger_noise_v} V rms; a channel off the board reads 0 V. To record: key the length in seconds ({logger_record_s_text}, two digits at most; CLR clears the entry), press RATE to step through {logger_rates_hz_text} Hz (120 at the start), then REC. The record starts at once and runs its full length; keys do nothing while it runs. At most {logger_max_records} records per episode. ERR lights for a length out of range, when no record is left, or when the record would not finish within the budget. Record n is evidence `<episode_id>:logger:<n>` (NO on the display); `export_logger(evidence_id)` returns a finished record: CSV `sample_id, t_s, status, ch1_V, ch2_V` (t_s is simulation time since the episode start) with its SHA-256.
- *Observations.* Every `get_observation` is evidence of the state it shows: it returns `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`). Readings are taken from the displays in its images.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- `measurements` lists the readings you used, each naming the LED it belongs to and citing one piece of evidence: an observation with the readings its displays show (`V1`, `V2`, `A`, or `CH1` from the logger display), or a logger record with a window [t0_s, t1_s] and the mean of `CH1` and/or `CH2` over the samples in it. Values in volts and amperes. At least one entry has `used_in_fit: true`.
- Readings are genuine: every cited display reading is within {counts} counts of what that display showed in that observation (it must show a number); every logger window lies inside the record, holds at least 2 samples, all `ok`, and each cited mean is within {logger_mV} mV of the mean of those samples.
- Measurements are valid: for every entry used in the fit, at the observation (for a window: throughout it) every block on the board is seated (a block counts as on the board from when it is seated there until it stands in a tray seat again; blocks in the tray are judged only by leaving the bench in order, and moving them is not a change), the observation or window starts at least {steady_s} s after the last change on the bench (a block placed on or removed from the board, supply ON/OFF, a SWEEP press, a meter range change; the rising voltage of a sweep is not a change) and nothing changed during the window, every cited display has converted since the last change, every cited instrument is seated on the board, and an instrument (cited or not) seated on the board measures an LED: across it, in series with it, across a resistor in series with it, across it and the ammeter in series with it, or across the supply while a resistor is in series with it.
- X itself is measured: in at least one piece of evidence cited by entries used in the fit, X conducts (at least {conduct_mA} mA through it at the observation, or at some moment in the window) and the instruments those entries cite determine both its voltage and its current. Its voltage: a voltmeter or channel across X; one across X and the ammeter in series with X, with the ammeter read too; or one across the supply together with one across a resistor in series with X. Its current: the ammeter in series with X, or a voltmeter or channel across a resistor in series with X.
- Leave the bench in order before you submit: supply OFF, every block back in a tray seat labelled for its kind (V1 or V2 for a voltmeter, CH1 or CH2 for a channel, any W for a wire, otherwise its own label), the logger not recording, the robot holding nothing; and no loose item tipped over (the direction that pointed up at the start now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top).
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A block is seated when every pin is within {seating_pin_to_socket_m} m of its socket (two facing sockets of two neighbouring pads), the bottom of its base is within {seating_base_gap_m} m of the board surface, it is tilted at most {seating_tilt_deg}° and the robot has released it. Only seated blocks are part of the circuit; a block that lies anywhere else is open.
- A block stands in a tray seat when it is within {seating_tray_xy_m} m of the seat centre on the seat floor, tilted at most {seating_tray_tilt_deg}°, and released.
- The circuit is solved again whenever a block is seated or leaves its seat, the supply changes, or the ammeter changes range, and at every physics tick while the output sweeps; it responds at once. The LEDs stay at {temperature_C} °C whatever current flows and are not damaged by any current the supply delivers.
- Grasping follows device_calibration.gripper_mechanism: a block is held once both fingers close on its handle (or display head) and stop.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** Relative error e_rel = |λ_X,submitted − λ_X| / λ_X. Success when all conditions hold and e_rel ≤ {threshold_pct}%. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e_rel / {threshold}).
