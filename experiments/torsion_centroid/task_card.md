# Centre of mass and moment of inertia of a non-uniform sample

**Goal.** Determine the longitudinal coordinate of the green sample's centre of mass, `x_com_m`, and its moment of inertia about the vertical axis through the centre of mass, `I_com_kgm2`.

**Sample.** The sample is a green bar with a total mass of {unknown_object_mass_kg} kg. Its frame: the geometric centre is the origin, the length direction is the x axis and the end with a red mark is +x. The internal mass distribution is unknown; it is known to be symmetric about the longitudinal vertical mid-plane, so the transverse coordinate of the centre of mass is zero. Seven numbered mounting holes, each open from below to take the pin, run along its centre line. Each hole's number is printed in white next to it, and a white scale with a tick every 10 mm is printed along the bar. A handle for picking and placing stands at the geometric centre: a bronze post under a round knob. Do not open the sample.

**Apparatus.** A torsion turntable with a vertical axis, a blue reference disc of known inertia, a photogate with its recorder panel, a damper lever and a storage box. The turntable is dark grey; the torsion spring stiffness and the inertia of the empty turntable are not calibrated in advance. A steel locating pin with a chamfered tip stands on the turntable axis, inside a painted magenta circle. Each of the sample's holes and the disc's centre bore (open from below) fit over the pin. An object is mounted when one of its holes (or the disc's bore) sits over the pin and it lies flat on the turntable; nothing clamps it: it turns with the turntable by friction on its underside. A yellow post standing on the turntable can be pushed to twist the turntable; it then oscillates freely. The photogate (a red fork straddling the rim) sits at the turntable's rest angle, and a flag on the rim cuts its beam twice per oscillation period. The reference disc has a mass of {standard_disc_mass_kg} kg and a moment of inertia of {standard_disc_moment_of_inertia_kgm2} kg·m² about its central axis, and carries the same handle on its axis. In the submission, `load` names the reference disc `standard_disc` (blue) and the sample `unknown_object` (green); `empty` is the turntable with nothing on it; `hole` is the number of the sample's hole on the pin (null for the disc and the empty turntable).

**Storage box.** The disc and the sample start in a grey-green storage box fixed to the table. It has two seats, numbered 1 and 2 in white print; seat 1 holds the disc and seat 2 the sample.

**Damper lever.** An eddy-current damper acts on the turntable: on a dark pedestal, a carriage with a yellow post slides between two spring detents printed OFF and ON. A dark-red magnet on the carriage stands over a copper vane at ON. At ON the damper brakes the turntable strongly (a swing dies out within a few seconds); at OFF it has no effect; in between it brakes partly. It can be moved at any time; nothing locks it.

**Instrument.** The photogate is operated from its recorder panel: a dark console with 12 push keys labelled 0 to 9, CLR and REC (REC is red), and a display. Digits enter the record length in whole seconds (at most three digits), CLR clears the entry, and REC takes it: {photogate_record_s_text} s starts a record of that length at once; any other entry shows ERR and does nothing. A record runs its full length and every key is ignored until it has ended. A record still running when the budget ends is lost. The display shows a state word (IDLE, REC, DONE or ERR) and three numbers: SET S (the entry being typed, or the length of the record running), T S (seconds recorded so far during REC, the record's length at DONE) and NO (the number of the record being made or last made). The display is read only in the camera images: `get_robot_state` returns the robot's own state and carries no instrument readings. Records are numbered 1, 2, … in the order they are made. `export_photogate(evidence_id)` returns a finished record and its CSV (`transit_id, t_block_s, t_clear_s`): the times at which the flag blocked and cleared the beam, in simulated seconds from REC, with {photogate_time_resolution_s} s resolution; `evidence_id` may also be the record's number or "latest".

At the start the disc and the sample are in their seats, the damper lever is at OFF, the turntable is empty and at rest and the recorder is idle.

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- Rows marked `used_in_fit: true` each cite a different photogate record of this episode, and together cover either the empty turntable, the reference disc and the sample mounted on at least two different holes, or one of the empty turntable and the reference disc and the sample mounted on at least three different holes.
- Each cited record is valid: it ran its full length, the load named in its row rested on the turntable at REC and at the end, the object stayed mounted on the pin throughout (for the empty turntable: nothing pressed on the platter and the robot did not touch the turntable throughout), and it contains at least one complete oscillation period of consecutive clean transits.
- Each row names what was on the pin during its record: `load`, and for the sample the number of the hole on the pin. Its `period_s` agrees within 0.5% with the period the evaluator measures on the longest run of clean transits of that record.
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) is tipped over (the direction that pointed up when the episode started now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Tidy up: when you submit, the disc and the sample are back in seats of the storage box (any seat), no record is running and the damper lever is at OFF.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- An object counts as on the turntable when, over the last 1/3 s, it pressed on the platter with more than half its weight, the gripper did not hold it and no part of the robot touched it. It counts as mounted when it is on the turntable, tilted no more than 1°, its underside within 2 mm of the platter top, with the pin inside one of its holes. The load is taken when REC starts a record and again when the record ends; the mounting is checked 30 times a second during the record.
- A transit is clean when, from the end of the previous transit to its own end, no part of the robot touched the turntable (its yellow post and the pin included) or an object on the platter (one pressing on it then, or when the record started; touching an object elsewhere does not matter), the damper's magnet did not overlap the vane, and the set of objects pressing on the platter with more than 0.02 N has not changed since REC (nor was a held object among them). The record itself holds only the times; the evaluator keeps the bench's condition of every transit.
- The beam counts as blocked while the turntable is within ±0.0055 rad (0.32°) of its rest angle; the flag itself collides with nothing.
- A key acts once per press, when its cap is pressed down at least 4 mm (of its 9 mm travel); it acts again only after rising back above 2 mm.
- The damper lever's detent spring pulls it to the nearer detent; it counts as at a detent within 4 mm of it. The damper's viscous torque is in proportion to the magnet's overlap with the vane (full at ON, none at OFF).
- An object counts as in a seat of the storage box when the robot does not hold it, it stands upright (tilted at most {storage_box_seat_tilt_deg}° from vertical) and it is within 20 mm, horizontally and vertically, of where an object resting on that seat's floor stands.
- The display and the printed labels are visual only. The console, keys, display and its stand, lever, pedestal, pin and storage box collide.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** Absolute error e_x = |x_com,submitted − x_com| and relative error e_I = |I_com,submitted − I_com| / I_com against the true values. Success when all conditions hold, e_x ≤ 0.3 mm and e_I ≤ 0.5%. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − max(e_x / 0.0003 m, e_I / 0.005)).
