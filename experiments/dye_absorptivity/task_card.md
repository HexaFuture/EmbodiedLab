# Absorption peak and molar absorptivity of an unknown dye

**Goal.** Determine the wavelength of maximum absorption λmax (nm, integer as typed on the keypad) and the molar absorptivity ε at λmax (L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹) of the dye in the two stock bottles.

**Reagents.** Three bottles feed the dispensing station: `STOCK 1`, {c_stock1_uM} µM of the dye; `WATER`, deionised water; `STOCK 2`, {c_stock2_uM} µM of the same dye; both stocks are concentrated solutions, and the label values are exact. The dye is not named; its spectrum and the molar absorptivity of this batch are not published. Liquid can only enter a cuvette from the dispensing station; it cannot be poured from one container into another, so each cuvette holds what was dispensed into it.

**Apparatus.** A rack of {cuvette_count} empty cuvettes (teal collars; the cuvettes are numbered 1–9 on their rims, the rack wells 1–9 on their collars), the dispensing station (dark base) and the spectrophotometer (blue housing). Every place a cuvette stands (rack wells, the orange station seat, the spectrophotometer's cell well) is a raised square collar with a chamfered well: a cuvette lowered onto the collar centre stands in the well.
- Cuvettes: light path {cuvette_path_cm} cm, capacity {cuvette_capacity_mL} mL. A reading needs at least {cuvette_min_read_mL} mL in the cuvette. Liquid added beyond the capacity overflows and is lost. The minimum for a reading and the capacity apply to the liquid actually delivered, which differs from the nominal volumes by the dispensing scatter: a fill of exactly {cuvette_min_read_mL} mL can end up just short of the minimum, and one of exactly {cuvette_capacity_mL} mL just over the capacity. Each cuvette carries a filling funnel whose white rim is the gripper handle (the short neck on the rim is the filling port), and a stir bar.
- Dispensing station: each bottle line has a coarse button ({dispenser_coarse_mL} mL, dark blue) and a fine button ({dispenser_fine_mL} mL, light blue), the volume printed on each cap. Each bottle carries a paper label with its name (`STOCK 1`, `WATER`, `STOCK 2`), and each line's name is also printed on the station with its pair of buttons. Dispensed liquid falls through the spouts into the cuvette on the orange seat. One press dispenses its nominal volume × (1 + {dispenser_repeatability} N(0, 1)) within {dispenser_dispense_s} s; unless the same cuvette stands on the seat when the button registers and when the flow ends, the liquid goes to the drip tray. The green `MIX` button stirs the seated cuvette for {mixer_mix_s} s (removing it earlier voids the mixing). Liquids added one after another stay layered until mixed.
- Spectrophotometer: single beam, wavelength {spectrophotometer_wavelength_nm_text} nm (integers), bandwidth {spectrophotometer_bandwidth_nm} nm. It starts at an arbitrary wavelength between 400 and 700 nm. Type the wavelength on the keypad and press `ENT` (`CLR` clears the entry); each key's label is printed on its cap. `BLANK` (violet) stores the light through the cuvette in the cell well as the reference at the current wavelength; `READ` (red) shows A = log10(I_ref / I_sample) of the cuvette in the well. Each takes {spectrophotometer_measure_s} s. Without a BLANK at the current wavelength a reading uses the factory reference and carries an instrument baseline offset; after changing the wavelength the screen shows `BLANK` until you blank again. The screen shows the wavelength, the last absorbance ({spectrophotometer_display_decimals} decimals), that prompt, the digits being typed (with an amber `SET` mark), `Err` after an invalid entry and `----` while measuring, nothing else: the instrument cannot tell whether a cuvette is mixed, filled high enough or what the reference contained.
- Screen: a dark glass on the spectrophotometer housing with green seven-segment digits. One row shows the wavelength (three digits and `nm`; while you type, the digits being typed; `Err` after an invalid entry), the other the absorbance (a sign place and four digits, the decimal point after the first digit; `----` while measuring). Two amber marks: `BLANK` (the prompt) and `SET` (digits being typed). After a BLANK the absorbance row shows 0.000. The screen changes only when a key is pressed or a measurement finishes: after a READ the absorbance row keeps that result (to 0.001, the reading noise included) until the next BLANK or READ or a new wavelength. No tool reports what the screen shows; the screen is seen in the camera images.
- `SCAN` (lime key, its name printed on its cap): records a survey spectrum of the cuvette in the well from {spectrophotometer_scan_nm_text} nm in {spectrophotometer_scan_step_nm} nm steps against the factory reference (no blank subtraction, so the instrument baseline is included), in {spectrophotometer_scan_s} s. Scans are not readings and cannot be cited.
- Photometric model: stray light ≤ 0.1 % of the incident light. Against a BLANK, A_measured = −log10((10^(−A) + s)/(1 + s)), where s is the stray light as a fraction of the light passing the blanked cuvette, s ≤ 0.0012; reading noise σ_A = {spectrophotometer_noise_sigma_A}.
- Buttons and keys are pressed with the arm: they travel {buttons_travel_mm} mm and switch at {buttons_switch_mm} mm down; every cap has a raised pad.

**Readings.** READ results are read on the screen in the camera images; no tool reports them. Every `get_observation` returns an `evidence_id` (`<episode_id>:obs:<n>`; `<episode_id>` is `episode_id` in task_spec.json) bound to that moment; a reading cites the observation whose images show it.

**Tools.** `export_spectro_scans()` returns every scan so far (`scan_id, t_s`, wavelengths and absorbances; CSV with SHA-256).

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- Readings genuine: each reading cites the observation it was read from (its `evidence_id` from `get_observation` in this episode); at that observation the screen showed the result of a READ, and the reading's `A` is the absorbance shown, to the last digit. For the cuvette each reading measured you state `c_uM`, its dye concentration (from both stocks; 0 when it holds no dye), which agrees within {composition_tol_pct}% with that cuvette's dispensing history (nominal volumes, label values). A reading of a cuvette that had spilled or overflowed cannot be cited.
- Measurement valid: at least one cited reading is of a cuvette holding a stock or a dilution of it.
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) is tipped over (the direction that pointed up when the episode started now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Restore the bench: when you submit, every cuvette stands in a rack well (any well), the cell well and the station seat are empty, and the robot holds nothing.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A cuvette counts as standing in a well (rack, station seat or cell well) only when it is not held, its release complete, tilted no more than 10°, and within 4 mm horizontally and 3 mm vertically of the well floor.
- A dispense lands in a cuvette only if that cuvette stood on the station seat when the button registered and still stands there when the flow ends; otherwise it goes to the drip tray. A press for more than the bottle still holds dispenses nothing.
- BLANK, READ and SCAN measure whatever cuvette stands in the cell well when they finish; presses while one runs are ignored. Every BLANK discards the references stored at other wavelengths.
- An unmixed cuvette holding more than one liquid is read in one of its layers, chosen at random for each reading.
- A cuvette tilted more than 45°, held or knocked over, spills all its contents at once. Once a cuvette has spilled or overflowed, its dispensing history no longer describes what it holds, for the rest of the episode, even after refilling.
- A key or button registers once when its cap reaches the switch depth, and again only after the cap has risen to within 2 mm of rest. The keypad keeps at most 3 digits; an invalid `ENT`, or `ENT` while a measurement runs, shows `Err` and keeps the wavelength; `Err` clears at the next key press.
- The dosing lines, the spouts and the neck on each funnel rim have no collision; each bottle, with its pump and the riser of its dosing line, stands inside one invisible collision column.

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** e = max(|ε − ε_true| / ε_true / 0.02, |λmax − λmax,true| / 1 nm), ε_true at the true maximum λmax,true. Success when all conditions hold and e ≤ 1 (ε within 2% and λmax within 1 nm). Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e). G is 1 when every acceptance condition holds (including restoring the bench) and 0 otherwise.
