# Spring stiffness (encoder bench)

**Goal.** Determine the stiffness k (N/m) of the spring that supports the tray from the oscillation periods of the tray at {min_loads} or more loads.

**Apparatus.** A tray hangs from a vertical spring and slides along a guide. The stand also carries a white vertical scale graduated in centimetres. The tray is orange-brown; its public mass is {tray_mass_kg} kg; the spring's own effective mass is not known. {n_weights} identical weights of {weight_mass_kg} kg each, red cylinders with a brass handle on top, stand in a grey-green weight box fixed to the table. The box has six seats, each numbered on the box and each holding one weight upright; the weights start in seats 1 to {n_weights} and the remaining seats start empty. The weights are identical and unmarked; they need not be told apart (any weight, any free seat). Full-load tray travel stays within {full_load_travel_max_m} m. Weights resting on the tray lift off if it oscillates too strongly. The damping is small.

**Damper lever.** A damper lever stands on the table: a yellow post on a carriage that slides in a slot along a dark pedestal labelled EDDY DAMPER, with two detents printed OFF and ON. A red magnet rides on the carriage; at ON it stands over a copper vane and damps the tray strongly (an oscillation dies out within about a second), at OFF it adds no damping, and part-way it damps in proportion to how much of the magnet is over the vane. Move the lever by pushing the post along the slot; a spring detent pulls the carriage to the nearer detent. The lever is at OFF when the episode starts.

**Instrument.** A linear encoder on the frame reads the tray position: one channel `z_mm`, positive downward, zero {encoder_zero_below_start_mm} mm below the empty tray's starting height, {encoder_sample_hz} Hz, {encoder_resolution_mm} mm resolution, range {encoder_range_mm_text} mm. Recordings are made with the recorder panel on the table: a dark keypad console labelled RECORDER with grey keys 0-9, a CLR key and a red REC key, and a black display labelled RECORDER that shows a state word (IDLE, REC, DONE or ERR) and the fields SET S, T S and NO; the record lengths it accepts are printed on it. The display also has a live field labelled TRAY Z MM: the encoder's current `z_mm` in millimetres (same zero, sign and resolution as a record, one decimal, updated 10 times per simulated second), shown at all times whether or not a recording runs, and dashes while the tray is outside the encoder range. No tool returns what the display shows: read it in the camera images. Each key acts once when pressed down. Type the record length in whole seconds with the digit keys (up to three digits; SET S shows the entry) and press REC: a length of {encoder_record_s_text} s starts a recording at once and the display shows REC, the seconds recorded so far (T S) and the record number (NO); any other entry, or REC with no entry, shows ERR and records nothing. CLR clears the entry. While a recording runs every key has no effect; it runs its full length and then the display shows DONE with its length. A key pressed after DONE or ERR first returns the display to IDLE. One recording runs at a time. `export_encoder(evidence_id)` returns a finished record and its CSV (`sample_id, t_s, z_mm`; `z_mm` is empty while the tray is outside the encoder range), `t_s` in simulated seconds from the moment REC was pressed; `evidence_id` is the record's evidence id, its number (NO) or `"latest"` (the submission needs the full evidence id string that `export_encoder` returns; a number or `"latest"` is not accepted there).

**Acceptance conditions** (the evaluator checks these; how you meet them is up to you):
- At least {min_points} measurements marked `used_in_fit: true`, at {min_loads} or more different loads, each citing as `evidence_id` the full evidence id string of a different `export_encoder` record of this episode. `load_kg` must equal the load that rested on the tray during that recording (the empty tray counts as a load of 0).
- Each cited recording contains at least 5 complete oscillation periods of consecutive free-oscillation samples (see Simulation settings) with a peak-to-peak motion of at least {min_pp_mm} mm, and its `period_s` agrees with that recording within {period_tol_pct}%.
- Your submitted values must be self-consistent with the standard model of a spring oscillator, including the tray and the spring's own effective mass: the evaluator refits the readings you mark `used_in_fit: true` with that model, and your submitted k must agree with that refit within {self_consistency_pct}%.
- Before submitting, put every weight back into a seat of the weight box (any free seat): at submission each weight stands in a seat.
- Before submitting, leave the recorder and the damper as you found them: at submission no recording is running and the damper lever is at OFF.
- Leave the bench in order: when you submit, no loose item (anything not fixed to the apparatus) is tipped over (the direction that pointed up when the episode started now more than 45° from vertical) or off the bench (more than 0.10 m below the table top), and the robot holds nothing.
- Submit exactly once; the first `submit_result` closes the episode, even if it is invalid.

**Simulation settings** (how this simulation decides; the same at every exposure level):
- A key acts when its cap is pressed down at least 4 mm of its 9 mm travel; it can act again only after it has risen back to within 2 mm of its rest height.
- The damper lever counts as at a detent (OFF or ON) when its carriage is within 4 mm of it.
- A weight counts as in a seat of the weight box when the robot does not hold it, it stands upright (tilted at most 10° from vertical) and it is within 20 mm, horizontally and vertically, of where a weight resting on that seat's floor stands.
- The load of a recording is the set of weights resting on the tray throughout the run of samples its period is measured on (below); a weight rests on the tray when, over the last 1/3 s, it pressed on the tray deck with more than 0.1 N while the gripper did not hold it and no part of the robot touched it; a weight standing on another weight does not count. A change of load elsewhere in the recording does not matter.
- A sample is free oscillation unless, during its 1/120 s interval, a weight counted at the start of the recording pressed on the tray with less than 0.02 N, the robot touched (at any force) the tray, those weights or any other weight pressing on the tray (a weight the robot touches when the recording starts is not counted as load, but touching it still counts), the damper lever's carriage was more than 4 mm from the OFF detent, or the tray was outside the encoder range. The record does not mark such samples; the evaluator knows them.
- The evaluator measures a recording's period on its longest run of consecutive free-oscillation samples with an unchanged load, as the mean spacing of the crossings of that run's mean position with `z_mm` increasing (the tray moving down).

**Budget.** {sim_s} simulated seconds; every physics tick is charged (observing and exporting records are free). A command that needs more simulated time than remains is refused and nothing moves; once no time remains, a further physical action ends the episode and nothing is accepted after that, not even a submission, so submit before the budget runs out. The wall-clock limit is in README.md. One submission.

**Scoring.** Relative error e_rel = |k_submitted − k| / k. Success when all conditions hold and e_rel ≤ {threshold_pct}%. Reward = G × max(0, 1 − e_rel / {threshold}).
